Beat Em Up

Privacy policy

Last updated 22 November 2026

Beat Em Up collects no personal data. It has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. This page says what happens on your device, what Apple does when you play online, and what we receive if you write to us.

Who we are

Beat Em Up is made and provided by Tahir Erol, trading as T-Innovations, in the United Kingdom. For data protection law we are the controller of the little personal data described on this page.

Contact: t-innovations@outlook.com

What stays on your device

The game saves your settings on your device: sound and music volume, the soundtrack you chose, whether the music is on, the picture quality and the on-screen controls setting. These never leave your device, and they are deleted when you delete the game.

The game does not save your name, your age, your location, your contacts, your photos or anything else about you. If you hide player names (see below), that choice is saved with your settings.

Playing online

Online co-op uses Game Center, a service run by Apple. If you play online, Apple signs you in, keeps your Game Center profile and friends, sends invitations, matches players and carries the game's data between the players' devices. Apple does this under its own privacy policy: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

During an online game the players' devices exchange what is needed to play together: each player's Game Center nickname, which fighter each player chose, and where the fighters are and what they do. This passes through Apple's network. We do not receive it and nobody stores it after the game.

Your Game Center nickname is shown to the other players in your game (two to four people), cut to 16 characters. You can switch this off in the game under Settings, Safety and privacy, Player names: then your nickname is not sent and you see the others as Player 1 to Player 4. There is no text chat and no voice chat.

When it starts, the app downloads one small file from our support website. The file tells the game whether online play and player names are switched on, and which reported names to hide. Nothing about you is sent, but as with any website the company that hosts it (GitHub) sees the internet address of your device.

Your age range

Beat Em Up is rated 18+. On iPhone and iPad the game asks Apple for your age range (in short: 18 or over, or not). In some places the law requires this. Apple may ask you first whether you want to share it.

The answer is used on your device, at that moment, only to decide whether the game may start. The game does not keep your age range and does not send it to us or to anyone else. If you choose not to share it, the game remembers only the date on which it asked, so that it does not ask again straight away.

If you write to us

If you send us an email, for example with "Report a problem" in the game, we receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to deal with your message and to answer you.

We keep reports about online play, and a note of what we did about them, for up to three years, because the United Kingdom's Online Safety Act expects providers to keep such records. Other messages are deleted when they have been dealt with.

Our email is provided by Microsoft (Outlook), which may store messages outside the United Kingdom.

What we share

We do not sell or share personal data. The only exception: if a report suggests that someone is in danger or that a crime has been committed, we may pass it to the police or another authority, or to Apple.

Children

The game is for adults (18+). It is not aimed at children and we do not knowingly collect any data from children.

Your rights

Under data protection law (the UK GDPR and, in the European Union, the GDPR) you can ask us for a copy of the personal data we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, and object to how we use it. Since the game collects nothing, in practice this concerns emails you sent us. Write to t-innovations@outlook.com.

The legal basis for handling your emails is our legitimate interest in answering you and, for reports about online play, our legal obligations.

If you are unhappy with our answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office in the United Kingdom (https://ico.org.uk) or to the data protection authority where you live.

Changes

If the game changes in a way that affects your privacy, this page will be updated before the change is released, and the date at the top will change.
